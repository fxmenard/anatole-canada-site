My Canada Page

"Life is like a book, and we only ever have it open at one page. Going to Canada, I get to turn to another one."

Part One
How this started
November

The question that came out of nowhere

My older brother went to Cambridge for three months back in 2020. School, a host family, all of it. He came back different. More sure of himself. One day in November, out of nowhere, I asked my dad if I could do something like that too. I think it caught him off guard, since I hadn't been talking about it before that.

I wasn't exactly easy to live with around then either. I was angry a lot, mostly at him. So I get why he hesitated. But he asked around, and people told him to trust me. So he did.

December

Sebastian's family, and a wall we couldn't get past

The first family we found was Sebastian's, in Portland. We clicked with them right away, and they were genuinely excited to have me. But the school Nico went to wasn't officially approved to host a foreign student, and there was no way around the visa rules. So that door closed.

February – March

Three families, and a lucky coincidence

Through the same network, CISV, we found three families in Victoria, on Vancouver Island. All three felt right, which made it a hard choice. Turned out they all knew each other well already, which made everything simpler. In the end, the project came together with Amy and Kai's family, who were genuinely excited about the whole thing. Kai has three older sisters and no brother, so I think he was looking forward to that part too.

June 19–23

Sebastian's family comes to Nice

Even though the Portland plan didn't work out, we stayed close. Sebastian's whole family came down for five days. We ourselves had just rolled in early that same morning on the night train from Paris. No car the whole visit, just trains, buses, and a lot of walking.

Day one — getting in

Low-key first evening: our own small apartment, with a surprisingly big terrace, dinner outside, and the city lighting up below us as the sky went dark.

Our terrace, first evening.

Day two — old Nice

The morning market, wandering the old town, climbing on some very old city walls, socca for lunch, an incredible waterfall, and a proper view over the whole bay from up high.

Market, old town, socca, the waterfall, and ice cream to finish.

Day three — the river

Early train out of Nice, then a full day at a river, swimming, jumping off rocks into cold green pools, and generally not wanting to leave.

A whole day at the river.

Day four — Villefranche

Sebastian's family had moved to Villefranche for their last night, so we joined them there and made it out to the Saint-Jean-Cap-Ferrat peninsula too: the old alleys, the citadel, a swim off the rocks, pizza on the marina at sunset, and a World Cup match watched from a packed outdoor bar that evening.

Villefranche, the peninsula, and a World Cup night.

Day five — goodbye, for now

One last meetup in the old town, then Sebastian's family flew off to Bordeaux and then Strasbourg, the next legs of their own trip. They're still very much part of this project, and they're hoping to see me at some point during the stay.

Bags packed, saying goodbye.

July

Meeting Marco before I even left

Marco, from Carmen and Flavio's family, was in Europe that month to see his grandfather in Switzerland. His dad drove him down to a camp near Dijon, run by a place called Maison de Courcelles, and that's where our two families finally got to meet. It was in a campground. Main event was an accrobranche, ropes course in the trees, and I loved it. Got along well with the other kids there too, there was a small pool, and we played a ridiculous amount of board games.

July

A video, for the record

Right after the camp, I recorded a video explaining the whole project, in English, and saying I'd cook some recipes from home once I got to Canada. Half of the point was just to see how much my English changes over the next few months.

Late August

Saying goodbye, Paris style

My dad put together a family escape game around Montmartre for our last days together. We also went down into the catacombs with my brother and sister. And I spent one whole afternoon just talking with Méline and Dune, my closest friends, before leaving.

This one's actually from the other end of the trip. More on that below.

Honestly, I was excited about the weirdest thing: coming back to school after seven months and seeing everyone's face when they realize how long I was gone.
Part Two
Getting here
August 31

Wheels up, and landing the same day

Said the actual goodbyes this time, at the airport. Then a long flight, two connections inside Canada, and somewhere over the ocean it started to feel real. With the time difference, I landed in Victoria on the same day I left Paris, which still messes with my head a bit. Flew the whole way alone, pretty unusual for a 13-year-old, but it went fine.

Kai's family, Amy and Paul, was supposed to be my host family from day one, but they were still away, so Carmen, Flavio, Marco and Emily picked me up instead and took me in first. Carmen herself wasn't even there, she was in Vancouver for work. My suitcase got lost somewhere between planes and showed up a little later than I did, but it made it.

Landed. With my host brother, Victoria airport.

September 2

The Malahat Skywalk

Day two and already up a 40-metre spiral tower with a glass floor, looking straight down at the water and the forest. There's a whole story woven in about the local Indigenous cultures too. Good first outing.

On the glass floor of the Skywalk.

September 3–4

Orientation, and skipping the big group trip

Paperwork took up most of the 3rd. On the 4th I went with Marco to the one orientation session run by VIE, the organization behind the exchange. There was also a group excursion on offer with the other exchange students: a few days in the Rockies. I passed on it.

"I'll do nothing. It's too expensive, and it's only high school students there anyway."Me, apparently

September 4

Carmen flies back, solo

Carmen had been away in Vancouver for work this whole time. Flavio was already here with me. She flew back alone by helicopter. Not really my story to tell, but the view was worth stealing for this page.

Carmen's helicopter ride back from Vancouver.
