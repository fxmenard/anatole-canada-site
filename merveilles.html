Three days in the Valley of Wonders

While Anatole is living his own adventure in Victoria, the rest of the family went hiking in the Mercantour mountains, above Nice. This is Dad's account, written for Anatole to read when he is back.

Friday 23 to Sunday 25 October

Missed train, borrowed boots

Rose and I missed the Paris to Nice TGV on Friday. By some miracle we got on a train two hours later. On Saturday we picked up a pile of hiking gear from my uncle Jacques and his wife Carole in Nice, including a pair of walking boots for Céleste (this matters, see below).

That evening Rose and I went to an astronomy session with my cousin Claire and her family, back at 11 pm. Céleste had just arrived from Strasbourg. We packed until 2 am because the train left at 8:30 the next morning. Cécile was in Nice but had to give up on coming because of a sore hip. She still helped with the organisation, as always.

Cécile and I went shopping for lunches and snacks, and she helped here too. Standing in front of the instant pasta and soup shelves is harder than it sounds. Verdict: Royco for the soups, Intermarché for the pasta. I also had the bad idea of buying shrimp pasta. Remember that.

Sunday 25 October · Day 1

Up the mine valley to the refuge

Train to Saint-Dalmas-de-Tende, then hitchhiking to the Lac des Mesches. We started walking at 11:10, which was more than we had hoped for. The climb follows the vallon de la Minière, with a superb torrent on the right and waterfalls dropping a hundred metres.

We reached the refuge at 2:20 pm, a lovely time to be done for the day. So we ate a bit late, and Céleste was starving: a homemade courgette tart and the tabbouleh Cécile had prepared with love.

We chatted with Alexandre, one of the refuge's two wardens (the other is Régis), and he told us about the Cime du Diable. A few days earlier he had seen Corsica from the top. He knew exactly what to say to me. We decided on the spot to stay two nights and go.

Only 17 people slept at the refuge that night, very few compared to July and August, when with the bivouackers they serve around 120 dinners. It felt like a family house. At dinner we sat with a father and his two sons, who live in Germany. By digging a little I found out he lives in the same residence in Villefranche as a friend of mine, and that he runs the biosciences lab where I once worked in 1994. Small world. He wants his sons to work a summer at the refuge. Not won yet.

The refuge is resupplied once a week by 4x4, plus three helicopter drops per season, mostly for the gas.

Monday 26 October · Day 2

The Cime du Diable, and a view of Corsica

We left at 8:30 and saw lake after lake on the way up. As usual, Céleste and I picked up the best skipping stones along the path. Snow appeared on the second to last section. It was slippery and I suggested turning back, but we made it, and after a bend the profile of Corsica appeared. Magnificent.

There was no snow on the last stretch, so we went all the way to the summit. The higher we climbed, the clearer Corsica became, its peaks cut out against the sky while the island's top is 220 km away. I was so happy that Céleste and Rose could see it. I had seen it only once, decades ago. Magic moments.

On the way down from the summit we passed the upper Lac du Diable, frozen over, before a detour by the Pas du Diable. A family of chamois welcomed us. We had been wondering when they would show up. They stayed a long time watching us.

Monday afternoon

Soles that came unglued, and a shrimp pasta lunch

A small incident: the sole of one of Céleste's boots, lent by my uncle Jacques, started to peel off at the heel. It should hold, we thought. We kept going down, skipping stones on the lakes on the way back, hunting for spots sheltered from the wind because ripples ruin a good skip.

The sole kept coming off, and then the other boot started the same act. By the time we reached the refuge, both soles were completely detached. We asked the wardens for help.

We were back by 1:30 pm, perfect timing for the picnic. The shrimp pasta did not please everyone, and it was fiercely spicy. Céleste's lips were on fire for half an hour.

I thought the big loop of the next day was dead. With those boots we would barely make it down to the Lac des Mesches. But I had not counted on Alexandre. He asked Céleste's shoe size: 41, the same as his. He lent us his spare pair, and they fit Céleste like slippers.

The hot shower at the refuge costs 2 euros. Fair, since the gas is flown in three times a season. So, mostly as a challenge, we each took a cold one, at spring-water temperature. I would say around 7 degrees. I came out with a headache after the shampoo. The others have long hair and did not try.

We were supposed to sleep in a loaned tent because the refuge was being treated for bed bugs. The treatment finished faster than planned, so we slept inside, warm. There were ten of us that evening, very family style, and we even got to celebrate someone's 20th birthday. Early to bed, alarm set for 6:30.

Tuesday 27 October · Day 3

The grand loop: Merveilles, three big lakes, and a very long road home

We left at 7:50 and did not dawdle. Up to the Baisse de Valmasque by the Lac des Merveilles, then down through a few centimetres of snow on the north face, and along the string of three big lakes to the Valmasque refuge. A few dizzy passages above the last lake, the Lac Vert.

Picnic at the refuge, far better than the day before. Royco soups are good but you need two sachets, and the Intermarché pasta without shrimp is perfectly acceptable. A few skips, and off we went to catch the 4:52 pm train.

The path down to Casterino seemed endless, though brightened by a superb waterfall and two very unafraid ibex. We hunted desperately for a bit of signal to book an on-demand 'Zest' bus. Once we reached the road, at a military pace, we hitchhiked for an hour. Relief when a car finally stopped.

Tuesday evening

The lady from Saint-Dalmas

The driver was a small woman from Saint-Dalmas who talked a lot (well, I asked a lot of questions too). She was born in the village and went to school in the Mussolini-era station building, once a boarding school. She told us about what she calls the annexation of her village in 1947. Of Italian origin, she had to speak French at school from one day to the next. It still seems to be a trauma for her.

I told my uncle Jacques about it the next day, and I checked afterwards: the attachment followed a referendum, and 92% voted yes. So the lady was an exception when she spoke of annexation. That is only her view of it. The area had been the royal hunting ground of Victor Emmanuel III, kept by Italy when the County of Nice was attached to France in 1860.

The lady is also a walker, with or without her friends. At around 75, she told us, she had done the Vallée des Merveilles alone a few days before, roughly our days 1 and 3, but in a single day. Parked at the Lac des Mesches, she walked for ten hours. Hats off.

We caught our train in the end and arrived exhausted and sore at home. What a trip.
